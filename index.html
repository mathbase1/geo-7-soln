Find the angle x in the rhombus

The whole problem collapses to three angles at the bottom-right corner: two equal “outside” pieces and the 60° angle of the equilateral triangle.

Final answer: x=80°
The crucial information is not merely that the inner triangle is equilateral. Its side length is also equal to every side of the rhombus. That creates two extra isosceles triangles.

Original image and our labels

We will name the four corners of the rhombus:

A = bottom-left, B = bottom-right, C = top-right, D = top-left.

Let P be the inner triangle's vertex on side AD and Q its vertex on side DC. The third vertex of the equilateral triangle is B.

Thus the equilateral triangle is PBQ.

The required angle is ∠DAB=x°.

1

Write down all the equal lengths

A rhombus has four equal sides:

AB =BC =CD =DA

The triangle PBQ is equilateral:

PB =BQ =QP

The problem states that the rhombus sides and triangle sides have the same length. Therefore all seven displayed segments have one common length:

AB =BC =CD =DA =PB =BQ =QP

For the proof, the two most useful consequences are AB=BP and BC=BQ.

2

Use the isosceles triangle ABP

Because P lies on AD, the ray AP points along the side AD. Therefore

∠BAP = ∠BAD =x°.

We also know AB=BP. Hence triangle ABP is isosceles, with base AP.

Its base angles are equal:

∠BAP = ∠APB =x°.

The remaining angle at B is therefore

∠ABP = 180°−x°−x° ∠ABP =(180−2x)°.
3

Use the isosceles triangle BCQ

Opposite angles of a rhombus are equal. The angle at A is x°, so the opposite angle at C is also x°:

∠BCD =x°.

Since Q lies on CD, the ray CQ runs along CD. Thus

∠BCQ =x°.

Now BC=BQ, so triangle BCQ is isosceles, with base CQ. Therefore

∠BCQ = ∠BQC =x°.

Its remaining angle at B is

∠QBC =(180−2x)°.
4

Use the 60° angle of the equilateral triangle

Every angle of an equilateral triangle is 60°. Therefore

∠PBQ =60°.

At vertex B, this 60° angle lies exactly between the two isosceles-triangle angles already found:

5

Find the size of the whole angle at B

Consecutive angles in a rhombus are supplementary. In other words, they add to 180°. Since the angle at A is x°,

∠ABC =(180−x)°.

But the same whole angle ABC is split by BP and BQ into the three pieces just found. Hence

∠ABP + ∠PBQ + ∠QBC = ∠ABC.

Substitute all four expressions:

(180−2x) +60 + (180−2x) =180−x.
6

Solve the simple linear equation

(180−2x) +60 + (180−2x) =180−x 420−4x =180−x 240=3x x=80.

Therefore

x=80°
Quick check: each side angle at B becomes 180°−2(80°)=20°. Then 20°+60°+20°=100°, and the rhombus angle at B is 180°−80°=100°.

The whole solution in one compact chain

AB=BP ⇒ ∠ABP =180°−2x°, BC=BQ ⇒ ∠QBC =180°−2x°, ∠PBQ=60°, ∠ABC=180°−x°, 2(180−2x) +60 =180−x ⇒x=80°.

Common mistakes to avoid

Using only “rhombus”

A rhombus alone does not force x. The equal side length of the inner equilateral triangle is essential.

Calling AP or CQ a full side

P and Q lie part-way along rhombus sides. The useful full sides are AB and BC.

Missing the two isosceles triangles

AB = BP creates triangle ABP, while BC = BQ creates triangle BCQ.

Using x for the angle at B

Adjacent rhombus angles are supplementary, so the whole angle at B is 180° − x, not x.

Final answer

The required angle is

x=80°

The quickest idea is to split the angle at B into (180−2x)°, 60°, and another (180−2x)°.
